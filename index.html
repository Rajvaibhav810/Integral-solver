Aim

Aim

To understand and evaluate multiple integrals through symbolic/numerical computation and graphical visualization. The experiment aims to cover:

  • Double Integration: Evaluation of iterated integrals over defined planar regions.
  • Conversion into Polar Form: Transforming Cartesian integrals \((x,y)\) into Polar form \((r, \theta)\) for regions with circular symmetry.
  • Application to Area: Using double integration to compute the area of complex regions.
  • Triple Integration: Evaluation of integrals in three-dimensional space.
  • Application to Volume: Computing the volume of solids using triple integration.

Pretest - Prerequisite Knowledge

Theory: Multiple Integration

1. Double Integration

A double integral computes the accumulation of a quantity over a 2D region \(R\). It is evaluated as an iterated integral by holding one variable constant while integrating the other.

\[ I = \iint_{R} f(x,y) \, dA = \int_{a}^{b} \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} f(x,y) \, dy \, dx \]

2. Polar Conversion

When the region \(R\) is circular or radially symmetric, it is mathematically simpler to evaluate the integral in polar coordinates \((r, \theta)\) using the Jacobian.

Transformations: \( x = r \cos \theta \), \( y = r \sin \theta \)

\[ dA = dx \, dy \implies dA = r \, dr \, d\theta \]

3. Application to Area

By setting the integrand \(f(x,y) = 1\), the double integral physically represents the 2D planar Area of the region \(R\).

\[ \text{Area} = \iint_{R} 1 \, dA = \int_{a}^{b} \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} dy \, dx \]

4. Triple Integration & Volume

A triple integral extends this concept to 3D space. If the integrand is 1, it yields the spatial Volume of the solid \(V\).

\[ \text{Volume} = \iiint_{V} 1 \, dV = \int_{a}^{b} \int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} \int_{h_1(x,y)}^{h_2(x,y)} dz \, dy \, dx \]

5. Change of Order of Integration

Sometimes, integrating with respect to \(y\) first and then \(x\) is impossible analytically. By sketching the region and changing the order to \(dx \, dy\), the integral often simplifies.

Rule: The new inner limits must be derived as functions of the outer variable from the region's boundary equations.

6. Spherical Coordinates

For triple integrals over spherical domains, converting to \( (\rho, \phi, \theta) \) simplifies bounds significantly.

Transformations: \( x = \rho \sin\phi \cos\theta, \quad z = \rho \cos\phi \)

\[ dV = dx \, dy \, dz \implies dV = \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta \]
